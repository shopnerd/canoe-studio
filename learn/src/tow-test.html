Model tow test

Pull a scale model of the hull through a pool at scaled speeds, measure the drag, and turn it into full-scale numbers. It’s the only real check on Canoe Studio’s drag prediction, it costs about a weekend and $60 of electronics, and it makes a strong piece for the display.

What you get out of it
  • A measured drag curve for the hull, to compare against the app’s prediction (Analysis › Speed, drag & race time).
  • The hull’s form factor k, which the app currently guesses at 0.08.
  • Evidence for the Design Narrative: “predicted vs. measured” is exactly what judges reward.

How scaling works

A model at scale λ (1:4 means λ = 4) makes the same wave pattern as the full canoe when it moves at the full speed divided by √λ. That’s Froude scaling, and it’s why a 1:4 model is towed at half speed. Wave drag then scales with λ³.

Friction doesn’t scale that way. The model’s water flow is at a much lower Reynolds number, so its friction is proportionally higher. The standard fix (ITTC) is to split the measured drag into friction, which you calculate for each size, and the rest, which you scale:

CT,model = R / (½ ρ V² S)  →  CR = CT,model − (1+k)·CF(Remodel)  →  CT,full = (1+k)·CF(Refull) + CR

CF is the ITTC-1957 friction line, 0.075 / (log₁₀Re − 2)². The calculator below does all of this for you.

Build the model

  1. Get the shape. Export the hull STL from Canoe Studio and scale it by 0.25, or print the section templates at 25% and hot-wire foam between them. The model isn’t the competition mold, so 3D printing or CNC is fine here. Only the mold rule restricts machines.
  2. Make it smooth and stiff. Seal foam with a skim coat and paint, and sand smooth. Surface roughness adds friction the full canoe won’t have.
  3. Add a turbulence trip. At these Reynolds numbers the flow over a model can stay partly laminar and read too low. Glue a thin trip wire (about 0.5 mm) or a row of small studs around the hull about 5% of the length back from the bow.
  4. Ballast and mark the waterline. Add weight low and centered until the model weighs 3.6 kg for race load (or your case), and check it floats level at the scaled draft. Mark the waterline with tape.

Build the rig

Recommended

Motor spool + load cell

  • A 12 V gear motor with a PWM speed controller winding line onto a spool at the far end of the pool.
  • A 500 g–1 kg load cell with an HX711 board and an Arduino or ESP32 logging force, placed inline at the model’s tow point.
  • Speed from the spool RPM, or from phone video past tape marks on the pool edge.
  • Parts ≈ $40–60.
No electronics

Falling weight

  • Line from the bow runs over a low-friction pulley (a bike wheel works) and down to a hanging cup of washers.
  • A constant weight gives a steady speed. Time the model over a marked 3–5 m once it stops accelerating.
  • Needs a drop height as long as the timed run, like a balcony or diving platform. Calibrate the pulley friction with no model attached.
Either way

Keep it honest

  • Tow from the bow at the waterline height, so the line doesn’t lift or push the bow down.
  • A light guide line or rod keeps the model straight without adding drag.
  • Pool depth ≥ 1 m and a clear lane. Shallow water and walls add drag.
  • Wait for the water to settle between runs.

Run it

  1. Calibrate the load cell with known weights (coins or kitchen-scale standards) at 20, 50, 100 and 200 g.
  2. Pick 6–8 model speeds covering full-scale 3–7 mph (0.67–1.56 m/s at 1:4).
  3. Three runs per speed. Use only the steady middle of each run, and average the force over it.
  4. Record the water temperature. It changes viscosity and friction.
  5. Enter the data below. Compare the full-scale drag with the app’s table at the same speeds.

Calibrate paddler power

The app’s race-time estimate depends on the effective power per paddler, which is a placeholder until you measure it. Time a straight, flying-start run over a measured distance in calm water at a steady race pace. Read the drag at that speed from the app, and this turns it into power.

Enter the result in Analysis inputs › Effective power per paddler. Do it at the same load (number of paddlers) you race with, and repeat on a second day. Wind and current matter more than you’d think.

Scaling: ITTC-1957 friction line with a form factor, the standard Froude extrapolation (ITTC-78 principle, simplified). Model drag estimates come from Canoe Studio’s Michell thin-ship wave-drag solver, which reproduces Michell’s own 1898 worked example to within about 4%. Treat them as a planning range, not a pass/fail target.
